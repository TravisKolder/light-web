THE LIGHT WEB
A Kingdom Network of Truth, Trust, and Stewardship
A concept proposal for discussion with Travis Kolder

Prepared by John Gross

PAGE ONE — THE VISION
1. Why I Am Thinking About This
Travis,

I wanted to share an idea that has been developing in my mind, drawing from many of the things we worked through together during our House Of Friends years.

You were on the board when we were involved in projects and outreaches in Uganda, Ethiopia, and the Dominican Republic. Your experience in banking and your leadership in the Cedar Rapids House Church Network, along with your emphasis on accountability and sustainable ministry, helped shape how I came to think about the Kingdom of God and its return.

Over 25 years of African missions, I witnessed extraordinary generosity, sacrifice, and genuine Kingdom fruit. I also encountered Christian corruption that was difficult to comprehend.

One experience particularly stayed with me. An American organization had raised substantial funds for a supposed African orphanage. When concerns arose, I was asked to investigate while I was in the country. I visited the reported ministry address and found an empty walled lot. When I contacted the people involved and explained what I had found, they stopped responding.

The photographs and stories had created an impression of ministry that did not correspond to reality.

The American organization had been deceived, and the people who had given in good faith were left heartbroken.

That experience raised a question for me:

Could we build a better way for people to verify Kingdom work, protect generosity from deception, and help trustworthy ministries produce lasting fruit?

I have been calling the idea the Light Web.

2. What Is the Light Web?
The Light Web is a proposed digital network that connects trustworthy people, ministries, businesses, and communities through verified information, transparent stewardship, and accountability.

It would use technology—including artificial intelligence, digital identity, and potentially blockchain—to help people answer important questions:

Who are we working with?

Does this ministry or business actually exist?

Are the claims being made supported by evidence?

Where did the money go?

What was accomplished?

Is the work sustainable?

What happens when something goes wrong?

The purpose is not to create another social media platform or a Christian investment scheme.

The purpose is to make truth easier to establish, trust easier to demonstrate, and Kingdom work easier to evaluate.

3. The Foundational Principles
Truth and discernment

Claims should be supported by evidence. AI could help examine ministry records, identify inconsistencies, check photographs and addresses, and distinguish verified information from unverified claims.

AI would assist investigation, not pronounce spiritual judgment. Serious decisions would require human review and a fair process.

Integrity and demonstrated trust

Trust should grow through demonstrated behavior: completed work, verified references, responsible financial practices, and a history of keeping commitments.

A church title, a popular ministry website, or a personal endorsement should not automatically establish trustworthiness.

Stewardship and accountability

Resources should be used for their stated purpose. Ministries and projects should be able to document what they received, what they spent, and what they accomplished.

The network would encourage transparency while protecting private information belonging to donors, workers, and vulnerable people.

Kingdom return

This is the principle that has always meant so much to me in our ministry discussions.

The question is not simply how much money we raised or how many people attended an event.

The question is:

What Kingdom fruit was produced, and can that fruit continue to multiply?

PAGE TWO — HOW IT MIGHT WORK
4. A Kingdom Project Passport
Every participating project could have a digital record documenting its purpose, funding, activities, and results.

A project record might include:

The organization and local leadership responsible.

The need being addressed.

The approved budget and funding received.

Suppliers, purchases, and payments.

Evidence of delivery and completed work.

Independent verification where appropriate.

Progress reports and final outcomes.

Lessons learned and plans for sustainability.

For example, a school construction project could document the materials purchased, the local contractors involved, the building completed, and the school actually operating.

The network would distinguish between a claim, a document supporting that claim, and an independently verified result.

5. A Kingdom Marketplace and Supply Chain
The Light Web could eventually connect verified ministries with reliable suppliers, skilled workers, and service providers.

A ministry needing rice, beans, construction materials, transportation, or technical services could examine a supplier's documented history rather than relying solely on recommendations.

Suppliers would be evaluated on their actual conduct: prices, quality, delivery, honesty, and resolution of disputes.

Participation would be open to qualified Christian, Muslim, and secular businesses under the same published standards.

The goal would be to make trustworthy service easier to find and fraudulent practices harder to conceal.

6. Kingdom Return: Multiplication and Subtraction
I would like to explore a framework that measures more than money spent.

Multiplication: What new capacity, opportunity, or lasting good was created?

Subtraction: What suffering, waste, dependency, or recurring problem was reduced?

Stewardship: Were resources used faithfully and as promised?

Sustainability: Can the work continue after the original funding ends?

Restoration: When mistakes or failures occurred, were they acknowledged and corrected?

The network should not pretend that spiritual fruit can be reduced to a numerical score. It should document evidence, outcomes, and lessons so that donors and ministries can make wiser decisions.

7. A Practical First Experiment
Rather than building a large network or launching a cryptocurrency immediately, I propose starting with one real project that can be independently verified.

A small pilot could:

Select a ministry or project with trustworthy local partners.

Establish its needs, budget, and measurable objectives.

Create a public project record with appropriate privacy protections.

Track funding, purchases, suppliers, and delivery.

Document the results, including setbacks and lessons.

Invite independent review of the financial records and outcomes.

The first experiment would test whether better documentation and verification can make a real difference.

If it succeeds, we could explore additional projects, participating organizations, digital credentials, and eventually whether a blockchain-based system or digital currency has a genuine purpose.

Any token or financial structure would require appropriate legal, accounting, and governance review before funds were solicited.

8. Questions I Would Value Your Thoughts On
Travis, I would especially appreciate your perspective on these questions:

Could this provide meaningful accountability without creating unnecessary bureaucracy for small ministries?

What financial controls would you consider essential?

How should we define and document Kingdom return?

What safeguards would protect donors, local ministries, and the people receiving assistance?

Is there one project where we could test this approach on a small scale?

I am not proposing that we have all the answers. I am proposing that we explore whether the lessons we learned through House Of Friends and our mission work could help develop something useful for the next generation.

The Light Web — Don't merely verify the story. Verify the fruit.

An invitation to prayer, discernment, and practical discussion.
